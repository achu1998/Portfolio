Project: Human-Aligned Text Generation with Small Language Models (RLHF Pipeline)

  • Author: Ashween Ramakrishnan (A1866320)
  • Supervisor: Dr. Orvila Sarker, Adelaide University
  • Core Tech: Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face TRL/Transformers, LoRA, SmolLM2, Qwen2.5, PPO, Reward Modeling
  • Project Type: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) Research

This project implements an end-to-end Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) training pipeline tailored for lower-parameter Small Language Models (SLMs). By leveraging Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reward Modeling (RM), and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), the study demonstrates how resource-constrained environments can align language models to human preferences while avoiding hallucinations, safety violations, and policy drift.

3-Phase Architecture & Pipeline Workflow

  1. Phase 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) — Evaluated Qwen2.5-0.5B, SmolLM2-1.7B, and TinyLlama-1.1B on the Databricks Dolly-15k dataset using LoRA. SmolLM2-1.7B emerged as the optimal policy base with superior BLEU (0.0338) and ROUGE-L (0.2002) scores.
  2. Phase 2: Reward Modeling (RM) — Trained separate sequence classification reward models on human preference datasets (Full-HH-RLHF, RM-static, Synthetic-GPTJ-Pairwise) using binary cross-entropy loss.
  3. Phase 3: PPO Fine-Tuning — Updated the SFT policy via PPO with KL divergence penalties (KL=0.05) to ensure policy stability without deviating from the reference model.
SFT Baseline Selection
  • Best Base Model: SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct
  • ROUGE-L Score: 0.200184 (vs 0.146 Qwen / 0.110 TinyLlama)
  • BLEU Score: 0.033784
  • Hyperparameters: Ir=2e-4, max_len=256, LoRA r=8
PPO Alignment & Safety Outcomes
  • Optimal Pipeline: SmolLM + RM-static + PPO
  • Reward Score Gain: Increased from 24.25 to 41.25 (+17.0 reward jump)
  • KL Divergence: Controlled stability (0.2320 → 0.0926)
  • Safety Guardrails: Preserved strict refusal on malicious queries

Key Research Takeaways

  • Reward Loss Paradox: Models with the lowest loss during reward training (e.g., Synthetic-GPTJ) do not necessarily yield the best PPO optimization. Stable preference boundaries matter more than raw reward accuracy.
  • Structured Formatting: Manual output comparison proved that PPO alignment significantly improved code block formatting, helpfulness, and directness over standard SFT.
  • Low-Compute Feasibility: Validated that SLMs combined with LoRA parameter-efficient tuning make high-quality RLHF accessible without requiring multi-GPU enterprise infrastructure.
